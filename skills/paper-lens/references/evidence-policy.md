# Evidence policy

Apply this policy in deep mode.

## Source priority

1. The exact paper version being reviewed.
2. Primary papers and their official arXiv, proceedings, journal, or publisher pages.
3. Official author/project repositories for implementation facts.
4. Secondary sources only as discovery aids; verify their claims against primary material.

Do not cite a search-results page as evidence. Do not infer acceptance venue, priority, performance, or criticism from an unverified snippet.

## Traceability

- Cite paper-internal claims with a page, section, equation, figure, or table location.
- Link external claims directly to the primary source.
- Mark interpretation as interpretation; do not phrase it as an author statement.
- Preserve exact experimental numbers and units. Say when values were estimated from plots.
- Treat absence of evidence as “not reported” or “not verified,” not as evidence of absence.

## External verification status

Use `<!-- paper-lens:external-evidence:complete -->` only when the relevant literature comparisons were opened and verified.

Use `<!-- paper-lens:external-evidence:partial -->` when browsing fails, sources are inaccessible, or the search is too weak to support a literature-level conclusion. State what was searched, what could not be verified, and which report claims therefore remain source-only.

Never fabricate a citation to satisfy validation. A transparent partial report is valid; a plausible but false reference is not.

## Local PDF privacy

Do not upload a local PDF. Search by bibliographic metadata or carefully chosen topic terms only. Avoid sending unpublished titles or distinctive excerpts when the user indicates the paper is confidential; in that case, ask before external search or keep the deep review source-only and mark it partial.
