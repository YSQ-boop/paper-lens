# Deep-review contract

Preserve the completed quick section, then complete every deep subsection marker created by the pipeline.

## Claims and evidence

Create a Claims–Evidence matrix with one row per central claim. Include the claim, paper location, supporting theory/experiment, strength of support, and unresolved gap. Separate author claims from your assessment.

## Theory and formulas

Explain the 3–8 formulas most necessary to understand the method. For each, give its role, symbols, plain-language meaning, dependency on assumptions, and training/inference or code mapping. Give a small numerical example for 1–3 central formulas when meaningful. Do not manufacture derivations omitted by the paper.

## Experiment audit

Map experiments to claims. Inspect dataset fit, baselines, metric validity, hyperparameter fairness, uncertainty/statistical testing, ablations, negative results, compute, and possible confounders. Transcribe the key numerical rows needed for the assessment rather than relying only on screenshots.

## Related evidence

Position the paper against verified primary literature. Explain exactly where another work supports, predates, competes with, or contradicts it. Include links near the claims they support and list only works actually used.

## Reviewer critique

Test the strongest assumptions, identify overreach, consider simpler explanations, and distinguish fatal issues from fixable omissions. Keep criticism technically specific.

## Reproducibility and verdict

List availability of data/code/checkpoints, missing implementation details, compute requirements, determinism risks, and a practical reproduction sequence. Finish with separate scores for novelty, technical soundness, experimental evidence, reproducibility, and overall confidence; briefly justify each score.

Insert important figures, tables, and formula explanations beside the relevant discussion. Do not collect all visual material in a detached appendix.
