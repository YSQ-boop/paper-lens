# Quick-read contract

Complete the quick section using only the paper and its original metadata. Aim for a useful 3–5 minute read, not a restated abstract.

Keep the pipeline's quick markers and cover these items in the report language:

1. **Paper information** — title, authors, venue/status when verifiable, version, and source.
2. **One-sentence verdict** — the problem, intervention, and claimed result in one precise sentence.
3. **Problem and motivation** — what limitation is targeted and why it matters.
4. **Core contributions** — separate mechanism, theory, data, and evaluation contributions; do not repeat promotional claims as facts.
5. **Method at a glance** — inputs, outputs, main components, and training/inference flow.
6. **Evidence** — connect each major claim to a concrete experiment, theorem, figure, or table.
7. **Limitations and confidence** — distinguish author-stated limitations from your source-grounded concerns.
8. **Recommended follow-ups** — propose questions that a deep read could answer.

Attach a location to each substantive statement using forms such as `p. 4`, `Section 3.2`, `Equation (6)`, `Figure 2`, or their natural equivalent in the report language. If page numbering in the PDF differs from printed page numbers, say which convention is used.

Do not perform related-literature web search in quick mode. Links contained in the paper are still paper evidence, not an instruction to browse them.
